# Click-to-move

With Agraria OS active, left-click a ground tile to walk to it. A cyan bracket marks the destination. Another ground click replaces the route. Movement keys, controller movement, other buttons, map transitions, day changes and F8 cancel it; menus, scrolling and desktop focus pause it and resume afterwards. Shift+left-click retains vanilla tool/item handling. Set `ClickToMove` to `false` in config to disable this feature independently.

Furniture left-clicks always use vanilla pickup or placement and cancel any movement queue, including with Ctrl or Shift held. In decoratable interiors such as the farmhouse, furniture can be picked up from anywhere; outdoors, vanilla reach rules still apply. Rugs and all tiles in a large piece's footprint work too. Furniture which can't be removed stays in place without starting a route. While holding furniture, invalid or out-of-range placement clicks also stay with vanilla. Right-click distant furniture to walk over and interact with it.

Equip a milk pail or shears and left-click a ready adult animal to walk over and harvest it once. Hold left-click and drag over more animals to queue milking or shearing, including during a harvest animation. The route and brackets follow each selected animal as it moves; babies, sleeping animals, animals without produce and animals requiring another tool are skipped. Right-click dragging still queues petting. Petting, milking and shearing are distinct actions: pet and milk the same animal in either order, with one queue entry per action and no duplicate entries when crossing its other sprite tiles. All actions share the nine-entry limit. Pending harvests remember their selected tool and recheck the animal, produce and tool on arrival. Native animations, quality, animal-cracker bonuses, inventory limits, stamina and rewards remain intact. Ground clicks still move; Shift+left-click keeps manual vanilla tool handling.

Queue up to 9 interactions: each queued tile gets its own bracket color (cyan, yellow, orange, green, pink, purple, red, blue, white in order). Click 5 rocks and they are mined in succession; right-click 2 NPCs and both dialogues play in order into Local. While a route runs — including mid-swing during mining — plain left-clicks on tool/approach targets and plain right-clicks on interactables append to the queue automatically. Ctrl+click queues explicitly, including plain ground waypoints. Plain ground left-clicks, warps and Shift+clicks still replace (or bypass) instead of queuing. A full queue plays `cancel` and ignores the extra click. Movement keys, tool/action buttons, warps and day changes still clear the queue.

The Overview grid supports Ctrl-click toggles and Shift-click ranges with the same nine-target cap. Right-click a selected row for bulk Pet, Collect or another matching queued action. New overview batches optimize obstacle-aware walking order and interaction sides, append after existing work, and reject the whole batch if insufficient queue slots remain. Double-click approaches a single row. See [overview menus](overview-menus.md) for selection and bulk-action details.

Animal queue entries use faint, one-world-pixel outlines around the current sprite silhouette instead of tile brackets. The outlines follow position, animation and facing, including during milking/shearing. Separate actions queued for the same animal get nested outlines in their respective queue colors. Other targets keep their tile brackets.

Hold left-click on a rock and drag across other rocks to queue mining, including while the pickaxe is swinging. Fast strokes sample every crossed tile; backtracking and crossing multiple tiles of one stone clump don't duplicate queued targets. Release ends selection and the queue continues. The same nine-target cap and input/UI guards apply. Nearby targets are handled from the farmer's current position, including diagonals within vanilla mouse reach; only targets outside that reach start a walk. This also avoids repositioning between nearby collections and approach targets.

Right-click a collectible or unpetted animal and keep the button held while dragging over other targets to queue collection and petting. A single drag can mix ready machines (furnaces, worm bins, charcoal kilns, crab pots, tappers and data-defined machines), hand-harvestable crops, garden-pot crops/output and spawned ground items such as eggs and forage, livestock and farm pets. Nearby targets join the same queue as distant ones; each crossed tile is considered once per hold, including tiles crossed between cursor samples during a fast drag. Existing queued interactions are not duplicated; animals are tracked by identity as they move. Release the button to finish selecting; the queued collections continue. The nine-target queue limit still applies. Empty or processing machines, growing/dead/regrowing crops, bare dirt and other interactables are skipped during the drag. Scythe-only crops keep their normal tool requirement. Already-petted animals and sleeping livestock are skipped. Petting follows the selected animal, rechecks eligibility on arrival, and uses native friendship, happiness, profession bonuses, XP and pet gifts without opening inspection menus. Golden animal crackers, pet hats and butterfly powder retain normal single-click handling. Collection rechecks target identity and readiness on arrival and uses native collection/harvest actions, preserving inventory limits, crop regrowth, quality, rewards, forage profession bonuses, tapper production and hopper reloads without feeding or planting your held item. Crop pickup animations briefly lock movement; drag selection continues and the next leg waits for movement to resume. Moving manually, Shift, another action, a menu, UI chrome, F8, leaving the game window, a warp or a day change ends the drag. Shift+right-click and disabled click-to-move keep vanilla handling.

Click the last visible tile in a map exit to continue across its boundary and warp normally. This includes the farm's bus stop/forest/backwoods exits and farmhouse doorways whose triggers sit one tile beyond the map. Clicking directly on a warp trigger also works. Other edge tiles remain ordinary movement destinations.

Click a solid tile or object, such as a chest, fridge or furnace, to walk to the closest reachable tile beside it and face it. Furniture uses left-click for decoration and right-click for approach/interaction. Multi-tile objects use their full ground footprint. The built-in farmhouse fridge accepts either its upper or lower sprite tile. Right-click keeps its existing interactions after you arrive. Unreachable left-clicks walk as close as possible toward the clicked tile instead of playing `cancel`, so repeated clicks step around dense trees and fences; only a fully stuck position with no closer walk still plays `cancel`.

Clicking a supported tool target walks to the closest reachable cardinally adjacent tile, selects the appropriate tool from the backpack and swings until that target is gone (up to 30 swings), then continues with the queue. Single clicks still do one target fully: click again only for another rock/tree. Water refills stay single-use. Tool animations, stamina costs, upgrade requirements, drops and multiplayer synchronization remain the game's responsibility.

Supported targets:

- Untapped wild trees, their stumps, twigs, rocks, weeds and grass.
- Large stumps, logs, boulders, meteorites, giant crops and green rain clumps. Any tile in the clump works; the hit is aimed at its reachable edge.
- Dry crops which need water, plus dry tilled ground when holding a watering can, and dead crops for scything.
- Bare diggable ground when holding a hoe, and refillable water when holding a watering can.
- Fruit trees when explicitly holding an axe.

The appropriate tool must already be in the backpack. Chests, machines and tapped trees are not automatically dismantled. To remove one, equip an axe or pickaxe and left-click within normal tool reach; nearby paths also receive the equipped tool's vanilla swing. These manual clicks cancel the movement queue and use vanilla removal rules. Distant non-furniture clicks still approach, and Ctrl+left-click on non-furniture targets still queues. Right-click interactions keep their existing behavior. This first implementation supports walking on foot; mounted movement, NPC approach/actions, automatic harvesting and item placement remain vanilla. It does not reproduce the entire mobile interaction system.

## Implementation

`Movement/TilePathfinder.cs` implements four-way A* with integer coordinates, a 20,000-node budget and cached collision results per search. It avoids vanilla `PathNode` coordinate and path-cost limits. `TilePathfinder.FindClosest` runs the same search toward an unreachable click while tracking the visited walkable tile with the smallest Manhattan distance, returning the path there so dense canopies, fences and walls step closer instead of playing `cancel`. `Movement/ClickToMove.cs` supplies the resulting stack to the desktop game's `PathFindController`; its ordinary `Farmer.MovePosition` handles collision, speed and player warps. NPC-only controller warp handling is deliberately disabled. The search uses `GameLocation.isCollidingPosition` with `pathfinding` and `skipCollisionEffects`, plus live NPC hitboxes. A stalled route can replan three times, then stops.

`Movement/WarpDestination.cs` resolves exit clicks against the location's live player warps. The search permits only the selected trigger as an off-map endpoint, then vanilla movement activates the warp before checking ordinary tile collisions. NPC-only and location-disabled warps stay excluded. A removed/disabled exit cancels the route.

`Movement/ToolTarget.cs` resolves target identity without invoking destructive tool probes. The target and carried tool are checked again on arrival and before every queued swing. `BeginUsingTool` starts the native animation/event sequence; upgraded hoes and watering cans receive an explicit release for one uncharged use. Each queue leg checks native reach first and arrives in place without a movement controller when already reachable. Otherwise it runs A* from the farmer's current tile with live collision plus NPC hitboxes. Mouse tool reach includes diagonals; tools which support their standing tile can also act there. Stale, unreachable or wandered-off legs are skipped with replans and chase re-resolves.

`Movement/ApproachTarget.cs` shares perimeter and facing geometry with tool targets. It resolves solid objects, furniture, the built-in fridge, terrain features, clumps and buildings to their live footprint; unknown blocked map tiles use their four cardinal neighbors. A moved or removed entity cancels the approach. Ordinary walkable ground still uses its exact tile, and supported tool targets retain their tool action.

Input routing in `ModEntry.cs` gives desktop windows, context menus, Pelicom and native on-screen menus priority. `Integration/ModEntry.ClickToMove.cs` handles movement guards and the world-space destination marker. Routes and failures are per screen. A failure restores vanilla left-click for that screen until returning to title; other mods' controllers are never cleared.

`Integration/ModEntry.RockMiningDrag.cs` reuses the cursor sampler with a separate per-screen left-button stroke. `ToolTarget.ResolveRock` only selects pickaxe debris/clumps, and `ClickToMove.EnqueueRock` deduplicates active, repeating and pending footprints. Selection continues while the mod's own tool animation holds movement; starting an idle queued leg can be deferred until movement resumes. Both strokes check down or suppressed button state until physical release.

`Movement/MachineCollectDrag.cs` tracks the per-screen held collection stroke and traverses its cursor segments. `Integration/ModEntry.MachineCollectDrag.cs` checks SMAPI's down **or suppressed** state before the game update while suppressing vanilla held right-click repeats. SMAPI clears suppression on physical release; its down state alone becomes false on the tick after suppression even while the mouse is held. Trace logs record right-click input, collection starts, crossed collectible tiles and why a stroke ends. `InteractProbe.TryResolveCollection` supplies guarded requests for nearby and distant machines, crops, spawned pickups, livestock and farm pets. Petting requests retain animal identity and a live approach footprint, including when a moved animal requires a chase replan. Crops and ground pickups approach adjacent tiles; terrain crops and pot crops call the dirt's native harvest method, pot output calls the pot's collection action, and spawned pickups use the location's native pickup path. `EnqueueInteract` can defer starting an idle route while a pickup animation locks movement; petting waits for that animation to finish.

`Integration/ModEntry.AnimalHarvestDrag.cs` uses the same stroke sampling for equipped-pail/shears left-drag selection. Animal requests carry both entity identity and action kind, so pet/milk/shear deduplication stays independent during approach, deferred arrival and active tool animation. Harvest requests retain their captured tool and live footprint through chase replans. `ClickToMove` keeps the active harvest identity until the tool's native finish event releases movement; it neither repeats harvest swings nor calls `EndUsingTool` on livestock tools. Harvest callbacks aim at the selected animal's native harvest hitbox and check the native animal selector before beginning the tool animation.

Queue display entries retain the animal reference through approach, pending actions and tool animation. `Hud/AnimalQueueOutline.cs` caches alpha contours per texture/frame/ring; it draws transparent-interior contours using the existing world SpriteBatch, without changing batch, sampler or render-target state. Native livestock and pet draw transforms supply positioning, flipping and scale. Cached CPU geometry is cleared on returning to title.

## Verification

Built against locally installed Stardew Valley 1.6.15 / SMAPI 4.5.2. Headless checks compare A* to an independent breadth-first search over randomized blocked maps and cover large coordinates/long routes, search limits, changed obstacles, multi-tile approaches, tool selection and stale targets. These checks do not verify animation/rendering or an actual loaded save.

Exit regression checks read the installed Farm, BusStop and three farmhouse maps. They run vanilla's path follower with simulated farmer movement against the real warp collision API, confirming the extra step reaches east/west/north/south and farmhouse triggers. NPC-only/disabled warps and unrelated edge rows are also checked.

Solid-target checks cover single-tile machines, multi-tile furniture, alternative reachable sides, enclosed targets stepping closer, fully stuck positions still playing `cancel`, stale entities, ordinary ground, map-tile fallback, both fridge sprite tiles and tool-equipped furniture/fridge clicks.

Collection-drag headless checks cover stationary holds, backtracking, fast horizontal/diagonal strokes, exact tile boundaries, 1,500 randomized cursor segments, gesture reset and location/day identity. Collection requests cover adjacent/distant targets, readiness changes, missing output, replaced/invisible machines, direct native action dispatch and chest exclusion. Actual mouse input, inventory rewards and machine animations still need a loaded-save check.

Mining/reach headless checks cover actual SMAPI left-button suppression/release, active/repeating/pending rock deduplication, diagonal repeat and queue advancement with simulated swings, preservation of an off-center farmer position, distant pathfinding, stale targets, the nine-target cap, machine exclusion, nearby collection without a controller, pause guards and manual cancellation. These don't verify visible tool animations or native mining drops.

Crop/ground-pickup checks also cover removed/replaced pickups, placed-object exclusion, native pickup dispatch, growing/dead/regrowing/replanted crops, trellis and garden-pot crops, scythe-only crop exclusion, direct dirt harvest dispatch, and deferred/deduplicated collection during the pickup movement lock. The SMAPI input regression checks actual suppression across 120 ticks and release/repress behavior.

Petting checks cover moving target tiles, approach/facing updates, identity deduplication, replacement/removal, range and pickup-animation guards, native action dispatch, already-petted and sleeping livestock, chase callbacks, and farm-pet eligibility per farmer. Friendship rewards and visible hearts still need a loaded-save check.

Animal-harvest headless checks cover adult/readiness/tool eligibility, moving targets and chase callbacks, separate pet/milk/shear identities, active-animation deduplication, captured-tool selection, mixed pet/milk queues in both orders, deferred selection during tool animations, stale produce and removed animals, the shared queue limit and cancellation. These simulate tool animation completion; visible milking/shearing, inventory rewards and multiplayer synchronization still need a loaded-save check.
